Thesis Structure
Your thesis should contain the following sections in order:
Title Page:
Refer to Appendix II.
Declaration:
A statement that the work is original and the student’s own effort, signed by the student (Refer to Appendix III).
Acknowledgments:
Gratitude to supervisors, contributors, and supporting organizations.
A summary of the study, including objectives, methods, main findings, and conclusions. The abstract is typically 200-300 words, but this may change based on your degree requirements.
A typical abstract would contain 1-2 sentences about the background to the project, 1 sentence describing the aims, a few sentences describing the methods, a few sentences describing the results and 1-2 sentences containing the conclusion(s). Although the abstract comes immediately after the title, it is best to write it after you have completed all the other sections.
Table of Contents (Appendix IV):
Include all headings and subheadings with page numbers.
List of Figures (Appendix V):
Provide separate list including the titles and page numbers of all included figures. List of figures should follow the table of contents (numbered in simple Roman numerals). Ensure that each figure has a caption.
List of Tables (Appendix VI):
Provide separate list including the titles and page numbers of all included tables. List of tables should follow the table of contents (numbered in simple Roman numerals). Ensure that each table has a caption.
List of Abbreviations (Appendix VII):
All abbreviations used in the thesis must be listed in alphabetical order with their full meanings. First use must be explained in text.
Introduction:
Describes the background. Answer why your area is important. Give an overview of research and identify gaps. Clearly define research questions/hypotheses.
Objectives:
Include the general and specific objectives.
Literature Review:
Overview of existing research. Discuss key theories, summarize major studies, highlight gaps, and link to your research question.
Methodology:
Clear and detailed account of how research was carried out and approach taken to analyze results. Must enable replication.
Results:
Present a logical sequence of tables/figures to summarize findings. Avoid raw data unless advised. Focus on a cohesive "results story".
Discussion and Conclusions:
Analyze findings in relation to rationale and literature. Acknowledge limitations. Discuss implications for theory and practice. Provide genuine conclusions based on findings.
References:
A list of references given immediately after the text, starting on a fresh page. Use Vancouver, APA or IEEE referencing style as specified by your faculty.
Appendices:
Include questionnaires, raw data, ethical letters, or supplementary tables. Must appear at the end. Must be included in table of contents. Page numbering must continue.

Pagination
Use lower-case Roman numerals (ii, iii, iv, etc.) starting with Inner Title page (counts as i, but hidden). Numbering ends with Abbreviations section.
Arabic numerals (1, 2, 3, etc.) start at Introduction. Must be included on all pages of text, illustrations, etc.
Page numbers will appear at the bottom center of each page.
Pages must not contain running headers or footers.
Landscape pages must still have page numbers in standard portrait position.
Headings
Sections should be numbered using Arabic numerals up to 3 decimals. Subsections should have Roman numerals.

Tables and Figures
Captions for tables should appear above the table, and captions for figures should appear below the figure (Font 12). Avoid shading in table cells.
Numbering Format:
First digit represents main section, second digit denotes sequential number.
Table 2.1:
Table Caption (first table in Section 2)
Figure 1.2:
Figure Caption (second figure in Section 1)
